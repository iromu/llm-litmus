Monochrome Editorial

A magazine spread that removes the most seductive tool in the designer's kit — and proves that what remains is enough. Two inks. One grid. An argument, set in type.

Four Principles

Section 01 — Foundations
01

Contrast

Black on white. White on black. The only two decisions that ever matter on the page — and both of them are absolute.

Ink & paper, in every proportion
02 12pt

Rhythm

A baseline grid sets the pulse of every column. Words land on the beat; the eye never stumbles off tempo.

Baseline unit — strict, vertical
03 900

Hierarchy

Weight before color. Scale before decoration. When only one channel is open, you learn to make it sing.

Heaviest weight on this page
04 1

Restraint

One idea per spread. If an element cannot argue for its place on the grid, it is noise — and noise gets cut.

Ideas per spread, no exceptions

Anatomy of a Spread

Section 02 — Components
05

Grid

Two columns, three columns. One rule governs both: the gutter holds, and the gutter is sacrosanct.

Column count > 3 — forbidden
06

Type

Nine-hundred for the shout. Four-hundred for the whisper. Between them, the entire music of the page.

System faces only — set in place
07

Space

White space is not empty. It is the loudest element in the composition and the first to be cut. Never.

Negative area, budgeted like ink
08

Scale

From a 10-point footnote to a headline that breaks the frame, ratio is the only ornament permitted.

Type scale — steep, deliberate
09

Margin

The border is the page admitting its edges. A double rule, a hairline, and the honesty of a print plate.

Trim, slug & bleed respected
10

Silence

What is left out is the final decision. The reader feels the absence before they can name it.

Omissions, catalogued for the record

Color is a guest. It arrives, it dazzles, it leaves. The page remains — monochrome and unbothered, still making its argument long after the trend has changed its name.

— The Monochrome Manifesto, Issue 14
